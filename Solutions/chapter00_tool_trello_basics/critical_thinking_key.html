Chapter 0 Instructor Key: Trello Decisions

Use this page while groups work. Do not print it with the student cards. The student page is Chapter 0 In-Class: Trello Decisions.

Desired Results

Students should leave able to say these five ideas in plain language:

  1. A list is a stage (To Do, In Progress, Done). A card is one task. Do not name a column after a single job.
  2. Three lists show progress. One unnamed pile hides what is started and what is finished.
  3. A checklist breaks one card into steps. Extra boards or a wall of cards are the wrong split.
  4. Labels only help if they show on the front of the card. Hidden colors look the same on the board.
  5. When work is finished, move the card to Done and leave a comment. Do not delete it or make a second “done” card.

If a group still says “just make another list” for every card, they have not split a list from a card. Stay with Cards A and B until that clicks.

Answer Key

Correct move, why it works, and what to listen for
Card Correct move Why Wrong move Listen for
A Create To Do, In Progress, and Done lists Those three columns are the workflow. Tasks start left and move right. Keep one pile, or make a second board. Nobody can see progress. Whiteboard columns, not one stack
B Add a card (not a new list) Lists are stages. Cards are sticky notes. Set up Xcode project is a card in To Do. Name lists after tasks. Then you have no workflow, only a pile of job titles. Stage versus one task
C Add a checklist The ContentView card stays one task. Checkboxes track the small steps on that sticky note. Dump steps only in the description, or split into five new boards. Steps get lost or the board explodes. Steps on one card
D Apply labels and show them on the front Colors on the board let you scan Setup, Coding, and High priority without opening every card. Leave labels inside the card only. The board still looks the same. Show on front of card
E Move the card to Done, then comment The team sees finished work. The comment records what happened. Activity keeps the history. Delete the card, or make a new “finished” card in To Do. The board lies. Drag right. Do not erase.
F Share the board link Turn on link sharing, then copy the URL. Classmates can open it without your password. Send only a screenshot, or leave the board private. They cannot check the real board. Share at the top, then copy

Exit Tickets

They got it

  • A list is a column or stage. A card is one task, like a sticky note.
  • I move a card to Done so that the team can see the work is finished.

Not there yet

  • A list and a card are the same.
  • Delete the card when you finish it.
  • Labels only live in the description.

Debrief Script

Ask the whole class. Do not create a new board. Do not open Xcode.

  1. After Card B, is “Set up Xcode project” a list or a card? A card. Lists are To Do, In Progress, and Done.
  2. After Card E, if the work is done but the card stays in To Do, what does the team believe? That nobody started.
  3. After Card F, why is a screenshot not enough? They cannot move cards or check labels on a picture.

If a Group Is Lost

Use one analogy only. Sticky notes on a whiteboard. The board is the whole wall. Lists are columns drawn on the wall. A card is one note. A label is a colored dot. A checklist is the tiny to-do list written on that note. Drag the note to Done when you finish. Share means someone else can walk up and read the wall.

If they finish early, have them rewrite one junk card title. Change stuff or do it into a lab-style title such as Set up Xcode project.
