Chapter 0 Figma UI Challenges: Instructor Key

Do not share this page with students. It is the answer key for Chapter 0 In-Class: Figma UI Challenges.

Groups of four. About 20 to 25 minutes. Each group picks one challenge and sketches one iPhone frame plus one component.

Desired results

  • Students treat the screen as a poster, not a website with a hamburger menu.
  • They reuse one component for repeating cards or states.
  • They keep typing off the glass. Icons, chips, and stamps beat text fields.
  • They can say, in one sentence, who the screen is for in the first three seconds.

If-lost analogy

The Figma lab was a floor plan for one welcome screen. These challenges are still floor plans. The “house” is a fridge door, a speaker remote, or a washer. If a group is lost, ask: “What does Avery see in eight seconds?” Do not ask them to add more screens.

Challenge 1: Stolen Yogurt Court

Elegant screen

  1. Four fat name chips on top: Jordan, Sam, Riley, Avery. Tap who you are. No password. Avery taps Guest.
  2. Auto layout stacks 3–4 FoodCard instances. Huge food name. Small owner. A color stripe is the rule: green Fair game, yellow Ask first, red Hands off, purple Sam asked.
  3. Tap a card. One giant bottom button changes: Ask to eat, This is mine, or I ate it — IOU. The IOU stamps the card. New leftover = six food icons, not a keyboard.
Stolen Yogurt Court: listen for
Clumsy Why it fails What to praise
Grocery list, hamburger menu, create account Avery slams the door. Wet hands cannot type. Stripe you can read from the hallway. One action, not a toolbar.

Challenge 2: Aux Cord Treaty

Elegant screen

  1. Top half is now playing. Song title, then whose turn in even bigger type (“DJ’s song”).
  2. One fat Skip (1 left) that goes gray at Skip (0 left).
  3. Four SongCard instances. Roommate name is line one. Song is line two. Screen order is turn order.
  4. Full-width bar at the bottom, always visible: RA in the hall — quiet.
Aux Cord Treaty: listen for
Clumsy Why it fails What to praise
Mini Spotify with a search box and tiny skip The fight is already happening. Search kills the vibe. The late roommate is lost. Whose turn is the biggest type. Skip budget on the button. Quiet hours never hides.

Challenge 3: Laundry Hostage

Elegant screen

  1. Top half is one huge state: RUNNING, DONE, or ABANDONED. Abandoned reads Sitting 3 hours.
  2. Under it: xX_Kyle_Xx and the timer. The fake name still counts.
  3. Abandoned: two half-screen buttons, Move to Dryer 3 and I’ll wait. After a move, stamp Clothes → Dryer 3 · Sam · 9:14 p.m.
  4. Running is only a countdown. Free is name chips plus Start my load.
Laundry Hostage: listen for
Clumsy Why it fails What to praise
Campus laundry map and a 12-step account form Hands are full. They need this washer, not the building. Status you can read from the doorway. The move button already names the dryer.

Exit tickets

Good vs not-there-yet
Sentence Good Not there yet
We used a component so that when the same block appears more than once… …we can change the master and every card or state updates. …it looks nicer / Figma wanted one.
A screen someone has to get in a few seconds should not… …hide the next step in a menu, ask for an account, or make people type. …be ugly / use too many colors.

Debrief script (about 4 minutes)

  1. Ask one fridge group: “How does Avery know in eight seconds?” Push them to point at a stripe or a huge label, not a paragraph.
  2. Ask one aux group: “Where is skip fairness?” The number should be on the button.
  3. Ask one laundry group: “If Kyle comes back, how do they find the clothes?” Look for a stamp, not a map.
  4. Close: shared college stuff is a social contract. Hierarchy and states beat more screens.

Early finisher

Duplicate the frame and show one other state. Fridge: a card after an IOU. Speaker: skip at zero. Washer: after the move to Dryer 3. Same component. Do not start a second product.
