Architecture Lab 4: Access and Security

This is step 4 of 7. Plan on about 40 minutes with your team.

Introduction

You already named what the app keeps. This lab is about who may see it.

You will draw the people, the doors, and one lock in the same Figma file.

Work with your team for about 40 minutes. Then each person turns in their own zip.

Use the same Figma link as the earlier labs. Your reflection must be your own words.

Learning Objectives

After this lab, you will be able to:

  • Name the kinds of people who use your app
  • Show who is stopped, or who only reaches the lobby
  • Point to the drawer that needs the stronger lock
  • Name the smallest proof that someone is who they say they are

Key Ideas

Access and Security is the bouncer and the locks. The bouncer decides who gets in. A lock decides who can open a drawer.

A screen that everyone can open is a lobby. A private drawer needs a stronger lock.

Prerequisites

Finish Figma Basics. Your team needs a project you can describe in one sentence.

You need a free Figma account. Create one at figma.com/signup if you do not have one yet.

Lab Instructions

Part 1: Talk through the questions

Read these out loud. Answer them for your app, not for a sample app. Give this about 8 minutes.

  1. Who is allowed in? Name the kinds of people.
  2. Who is stopped, or only allowed into the lobby?
  3. Which drawer from Memory needs the stronger lock?
  4. What is the smallest proof that someone is who they say they are?

Part 2: Build the frame

Give this about 25 minutes. A classmate should understand the frame in 60 seconds.

  1. Open the same team file. Do not start a new file.
  2. Add a frame to the right. Name it 4 Access and Security.
  3. Add two or three cards. Name the kind of person, such as student or coach. Do not put a real password on the card.
  4. Draw an arrow from each person to the screen or drawer they may open.
  5. Add one lock mark on the sensitive item from your Memory frame.
  6. Add a short note that names the proof. A school login, a code, or a password is enough.

Your frame is done when it has all of these:

  • Two or three people cards
  • Arrows from each person to what they may open
  • One lock on the sensitive item
  • A short note that names the proof, such as a school login or a code

Part 3: Share and export

  1. Click Share in the top right.
  2. Set access to Anyone with the link can view.
  3. Copy the link. You will paste it in links.txt.
  4. Select the frame named 4 Access and Security.
  5. In the properties panel, scroll to Export. Click +. Choose PNG at 1x.
  6. Export the frame. Save it as architecture-4-access.png.

Put that PNG in a folder named screenshots. The file name must match exactly.

Reflection Questions

Answer in your own words. Write at least two sentences for each question. Do not copy a teammate.

  1. Who is allowed in, and who is stopped?
  2. What item has the stronger lock?
  3. What happens if the wrong person gets in?

Deliverables

Submit one zip file named architecture4-access-lastname-firstname.zip. The team may share one Figma link. Each student still writes their own reflection.

  • links.txt with one line: Figma: and your view link
  • reflections.txt with Q1:, Q2:, and Q3:
  • screenshots/architecture-4-access.png

Troubleshooting

Share link asks people to log in

Click Share. Set access to Anyone with the link can view. Copy that link into links.txt.

The export is blank or cropped

Select the outer frame named 4 Access and Security. Do not select a text box inside it.

Every person can open every drawer

Then you do not have a lock yet. Pick one item that a stranger must not see.

The proof is “they just know”

Name a real check. A login, a code, or a teacher approval is a proof. A guess is not.

How to Submit on Canvas

Package your work as one zip file. Use the exact names in the Architecture Lab 4 submission guide.

Create your zip on a Mac

  1. Create a folder named architecture4-access-lastname-firstname.
  2. Put links.txt, reflections.txt, and the screenshots folder inside it.
  3. Right-click the folder and choose Compress.
  4. Rename the zip if needed, then upload it to this Canvas assignment.

Compress the folder, not the loose files. See the Mac zip steps if you get stuck.
